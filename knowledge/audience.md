# Audience & Buyer Personas

Every piece of content should be written for a specific one of these people, not a
generic "homebuyer." When starting a new piece, name the persona it's for in
`inputs/current-topic.md` and write directly to them.

## 1. The New York-to-Florida Relocator

**Who they are:** High earner or high-net-worth individual/family currently based in
New York (Manhattan, Brooklyn, Westchester, Long Island) actively considering or
committed to relocating to South Florida, often driven by tax strategy, lifestyle, space,
or weather.

**What they're thinking:**
- "Is this a lifestyle downgrade or upgrade?" — they need reassurance FL delivers on
  culture, food, and sophistication, not just sun.
- Tax and financial motivations (no state income tax) paired with genuine anxiety about
  leaving their NY network and identity.
- Logistics anxiety: remote buying, timing the sale of their NY property against a FL
  purchase, insurance and HOA differences, hurricane concerns.
- Status and image — they still want a home that signals the same caliber of success
  their NY life did.

**Content angles that work:** side-by-side lifestyle comparisons, "what a New Yorker
needs to know before buying in Fort Lauderdale/Miami," remote/sight-unseen buying
process content, insurance and tax literacy content, neighborhood breakdowns framed
against NYC neighborhoods they already understand (e.g., "Las Olas is Fort Lauderdale's
West Village").

**Objections to address in content:** insurance costs, HOA rules, hurricane risk,
"nothing to do here" myth, missing their social circle.

## 2. The Waterfront / Boating Lifestyle Buyer

**Who they are:** Buyer prioritizing direct water access, dockage, and boating
lifestyle — may already own a boat or be buying into the lifestyle for the first time.

**What they're thinking:** Deed restrictions, dock length/depth, no-fixed-bridge ocean
access, proximity to inlets, flood/insurance realities, resale strength of true
waterfront vs. canal-view.

**Content angles that work:** explainers on what "no fixed bridge" means and why it
matters, dock and draft specifics tied to real listings, day-in-the-life content (boat
to restaurant, boat to open water), waterfront-specific market data (see
`knowledge/markets.md`).

## 3. The Las Olas / Las Olas Isles / Rio Vista Lifestyle Buyer

**Who they are:** Buyer drawn to walkability, restaurant and gallery culture, and a
close-in, architecturally distinct neighborhood rather than a suburban master-planned
community.

**What they're thinking:** Wants urban energy without giving up water, wants character
and history (Rio Vista's older housing stock) balanced against renovation realities,
cares about walkability to Las Olas Boulevard.

**Content angles that work:** neighborhood guides (see `prompts/neighborhood-guide.md`),
architecture and renovation content, "a day on Las Olas" lifestyle content, historic
home due-diligence education for Rio Vista specifically.

## 4. The Miami / Sunny Isles Luxury Condo Buyer or Investor

**Who they are:** Buyer or investor targeting new-construction or resale high-rise
condos, often international or out-of-state, sometimes purchasing as a second home,
rental investment, or pied-à-terre.

**What they're thinking:** Building amenities and brand (branded residences), rental
policy and investment yield, HOA/condo fee transparency post-reform legislation,
developer reputation, views and floor-plan efficiency.

**Content angles that work:** building-by-building breakdowns, condo financial
health/reserve literacy (a real post-Surfside concern — handle with real data only, see
`knowledge/compliance.md`), investment yield framing (only with real numbers), pre-
construction vs. resale tradeoffs.

## 5. The Luxury Seller

**Who they are:** Owner of a high-value property (waterfront home, Las Olas-area
property, luxury condo) evaluating whether and how to sell, and who will sell it for
them.

**What they're thinking:** What is my home actually worth right now, what does Glen's
marketing and negotiation process look like, will my privacy be protected, how is this
agent different from the last one who sold a similar property, timing the market.

**Content angles that work:** listing launch case-study style content (once real
results exist), marketing process transparency (what Glen actually does — photography,
video, PR, database, agent network), market update content that positions Glen as the
one tracking the data closely (`prompts/market-update.md`).

## 6. The Investor / Second-Home Buyer

**Who they are:** Buyer treating the purchase partly or fully as a financial decision —
rental income, appreciation, portfolio diversification, or a seasonal second home.

**What they're thinking:** Cap rate and rental comps (real numbers only), carrying costs
(HOA, insurance, taxes), management logistics for an absentee owner, exit liquidity of
the specific asset class.

**Content angles that work:** market data content, neighborhood-level investment
framing, condo fee/reserve education, seasonal usage vs. rental yield tradeoffs.

## Using These Personas

Every prompt in `prompts/` should specify (or ask) which persona the content is for.
Writing "to everyone" produces generic content — writing to one specific person in one
of these six groups produces content that converts. When a topic serves multiple
personas, create separate versions rather than one watered-down piece.
